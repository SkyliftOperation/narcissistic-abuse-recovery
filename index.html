The damage doesn't show as bruises.
It shows as self-doubt.

Narcissistic abuse rarely announces itself. It arrives as confusion, quietly eroding your certainty about what is real.

  1. 01

    You replay conversations for hours, hunting for the moment it became your fault.

  2. 02

    You apologise for things you didn't do, just to make the tension stop.

  3. 03

    You were called dramatic, jealous, crazy, too much. Eventually you believed it.

  4. 04

    You no longer trust your own memory of what was said.

  5. 05

    You still miss them. And you hate yourself for missing them.

  6. 06

    You got out, but the anxiety, the flinching and the self-blame came with you.

  7. 07

    You read everyone else's needs instantly, and can't name one of your own.

  8. 08

    You've done therapy, books, twelve-step, and something still feels unfinished.

None of that is weakness or over-reaction. Those are the fingerprints of narcissistic abuse, and they respond to the right work.

Why you can't just "get over it"

You're not failing at healing.
You're up against biology.

Four forces keep survivors stuck, and not one of them is a character flaw. Each responds to a different kind of work.

See how we work on them

The trauma bond

Unpredictable cruelty followed by sudden warmth creates one of the strongest attachments the human brain can form. Missing them isn't proof it was love. It's proof the bond was engineered.

Cognitive dissonance

You hold two incompatible truths: "they hurt me" and "they're the only one who understands me." Your mind resolves the conflict the cheapest way it can: by blaming you.

A nervous system stuck on

Years of scanning for the next mood shift leaves your body braced long after the danger has gone. Insight alone won't switch that off. The body has to be taught safety again.

Shame that was never yours

Their contempt was installed as your inner voice. Until that voice is separated out and named, every attempt at a boundary feels like cruelty on your part.

The approach

Healing from the inside out

Not traditional talk therapy, and not advice-giving. Structured, practical recovery work, done together and at your pace.

Loosening the pull, whether you've left, are planning to, or are still in it.

Teaching a braced body that the threat has passed, so calm stops feeling unfamiliar.

Shame healing & belief rewiring

Separating their voice from yours, and dismantling the beliefs it left behind.

Inner-child healing

Reaching the earlier wound that made the dynamic feel familiar in the first place.

Boundaries without guilt

Saying no, going low-contact or grey-rock, without collapsing into self-blame.

Ask on a free call
Do I have to leave my narcissistic partner before starting?

Not at all. Many clients start while still in the relationship. We focus on clarity, boundaries and inner strength. Decisions come later, and they come from you.

Is coaching the same as therapy?

No. Coaching focuses on growth, new patterns and forward movement. Therapy often focuses on processing the past. Both are valuable. Coaching accelerates practical transformation. This is coaching, not mental health treatment.

What if I've already done twelve-step or therapy?

Many of my clients have, and they come to me because something still feels unresolved. My approach fills in the emotional and spiritual pieces those routes often leave open.

What if I'm not certain it was "real" abuse?

That doubt is one of the most common after-effects there is. You don't need a diagnosis, proof or a label to start. You only need to know that something isn't right.

How long before I notice a change?

Clients often feel shifts within the first few sessions, usually in self-blame and clarity first, then in how the body responds to conflict.

Do you take insurance?

I don't accept insurance. The initial 20-minute discovery call is complimentary, and we'll discuss the details of working together on that call.
